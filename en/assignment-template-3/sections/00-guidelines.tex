\section*{Before You Start}

\begin{blockbox}{Rules, notes, and policies}
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Review the course slides at least once before you begin.

        \item Using AI tools for research and search is fine. Handing the
              writing of your final answers to one is not, and any question
              found to be written that way is graded zero.

        \item Discussing problems with your classmates is encouraged. Sharing
              answers is not, and is handled under the university's academic
              integrity rules for both parties involved.

        \item The assignment is worth \hwgrade{} point(s) of the course grade,
              scored out of 100 for precision.

        \item Cite every book, paper, and repository you draw on, at the end
              of your answers.

        \item The late policy is the one agreed at the start of the semester.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{blockbox}

\subsection*{What to hand in}

A single \texttt{.zip} archive, named and laid out as follows:

\begin{codeblock}{submission.txt}
@\coursecode@-HW@\hwnumber@-[STDID].zip
|
|-- @\coursecode@-HW@\hwnumber@-[STDID].pdf
|-- HW@\hwnumber@-Practical/
        |-- *
\end{codeblock}

\texttt{STDID} is your student number. The PDF holds the theoretical answers
together with the practical report; the practical files go in the folder.

\subsection*{Check before you upload}

\begin{checklist}
    \citem Every question answered, or explicitly marked as skipped.
    \item Report includes the packages you used and why.
    \item Code runs from a clean checkout.
    \item Archive named exactly as above.
\end{checklist}

\begin{asidebox}{A note on partial credit}
    An honest partial answer that shows the approach earns more than a
    confident wrong one. Write down what you tried and where it broke.
\end{asidebox}

Good luck.
